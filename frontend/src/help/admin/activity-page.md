## What this is for

The **Activity** page shows how much LabAdda is used: how many people were active, how many things they made, when the site is busiest, and how storage grows. It shows counts and sizes only. It never shows messages, notes, file names or board pictures, and it names projects and groups only as the totals **Projects** and **Groups**. Usage readers can see this page too, but cannot change anything.

## Steps

1. Choose **Activity** in the administration menu.
2. Choose a **Period**: last 7 days, last 30 days, last 90 days or last 12 months. Days are counted in the site time zone and end today.
3. Read the tiles at the top. Each gives a figure and says, in words, how it compares with the previous period of the same length.
4. Read the charts below: active people per day, activity by feature, activity by weekday and hour, storage growth and uploads by size.
5. Choose **View as table** under any chart to read the same figures as numbers.
6. Scroll to **Activity by unit** to see active people in each unit. The chart shows the ten busiest units and puts the rest together as "Other units". The table under it lists every unit.
7. Choose **Export figures** to save the figures you see as a spreadsheet file.

## What happens next

The figures refresh by themselves every minute while the page is on screen. Every export is recorded in the audit trail with your name, the period and the number of rows.

## If something goes wrong

If a figure says **Not measured**, nothing was collected for it in that period. This is not the same as no use: the server may have been switched off, or the figure is new. If the page says **Not measured yet**, choose a longer period or wait until people have used LabAdda for a few minutes. If a unit or a tile shows **Fewer than 5**, fewer than five people were active, so the number is hidden to protect their privacy. On a small site, days and weekdays with fewer than five active people are left out of the charts for the same reason. Another unit may be hidden as well, so that no one can work out a single person's activity by subtracting. Unit figures are rounded to the nearest five for the same reason.
