# Administration, observability and policy safety — future contract

Status: **specified for later phases, not existing functionality**. Builds on S2, DATA-LIFECYCLE.md and RECOVERY.md. The goal is a complete operational view with safe actions, not an unrestricted content browser.

## Roles and access

**AC-01.** Define explicit capabilities for identity/tree administration, policy drafting/activation, quota exceptions, hold management, infrastructure health, restricted audit, backup-job operations and recovery approval. Existing administrators migrate to a documented least-privilege capability set; no silent new content grant. New operations must check server-side capabilities and object scope on every request, including streams, CSV downloads, job logs and polling. Board-only sessions are denied all admin functions. Last-active-admin protection remains mandatory.

**AC-02.** Admin views may expose necessary operational metadata—account/scope IDs and names already permitted for administration, counters, policy sources, timestamps, status and sanitized diagnostic codes. They must not expose message bodies, note titles, personal search terms, filenames, board previews or attachment bytes without the same current content grant required in the ordinary app. Per-user aggregate personal-note usage is quota metadata, not permission to browse notes. Every sensitive metadata drilldown and downloadable report is auditable and permission-scoped. Workspace/group names are admin metadata as already allowed by S2.1; the restriction on personal content remains intact.

**AC-03.** Policy activation and destructive recovery/lifecycle controls require recent reauthentication, CSRF protection, trusted origin, narrowly scoped authorization, a reason, explicit target and fresh preview. Support two-person review for high-risk policy changes, retroactive retention, removing preservation holds, broad deletion, backup deletion and restoring/activating recovered state. Proposer and approver must differ; preview cannot grant either missing capabilities. Sites with only one administrator may use an explicit separately provisioned single-operator mode with reauthentication, delayed activation, a verified recovery prerequisite and prominent audit; it must not silently satisfy a two-person requirement. The console must explain the unavailable prerequisite rather than offer a bypass.

## Policy change state machine

**AC-04.** Use `draft → validated → previewed → approved → scheduled → active → superseded`, with rejected/cancelled/expired states. Every transition is server-authorized and audited. A draft is a complete versioned patch against an identified base revision; optimistic concurrency must reject stale edits with a human-readable field diff. Do not use last-writer-wins for policy. Permit download of a sanitized configuration snapshot/diff for offline review and disaster recovery; never include secret values. Any supported policy import follows the same validation/preview/review path and cannot immediately activate rules.

**AC-05.** Validation must check units, bounds, valid IANA time zones, permitted cron/window semantics, per-file vs user/workspace budgets, site hard caps/reserve, soft/hard threshold ordering, expanded archive vs available staging capacity, warning/notice/grace ordering, supported media renderers, minimum board-reconstruction windows, conflicting hold/expiry rules, exception expiry and backup/log/audit dependencies. Show field-specific errors and effective inherited policy with examples. Limits that prevent every ordinary allowed upload require a visible deliberate acknowledgement and evidence of the intended mode. Unlimited/high ceilings require a capacity justification and explicit approval; configurable does not mean unconstrained.

**AC-06.** A dry run is a bounded, read-only server computation over a consistent inventory generation. It must state scope, inventory age/completeness, base/candidate policy revisions, reference-graph/accounting versions, evaluated and affected counts, newly over-quota users/workspaces, proposed expiry/trash/purge sets, held/skipped/dependent items, estimated logical vs physical bytes reclaimed, temporary space needed, job duration estimate and confidence/unknowns, notification recipients, notice deadlines, restore window and recovery prerequisites. Content samples may show only metadata authorized to the reviewer; do not leak private titles/body snippets to make a preview attractive. Paginate large results, record truncation and provide a protected complete report. An incomplete or stale inventory cannot approve destructive action.

**AC-07.** The server binds approval to a cryptographic plan digest, exact candidate IDs/versions, scope, policy/base revision, inventory generations, reviewer and expiry. Show a human-readable change summary and typed scope confirmation for irreversible actions. Changes to policy/permissions/holds or material source data invalidate approval as appropriate; no hidden recomputation of a larger deletion set. Jobs recheck each planned candidate and skip changed ones rather than expanding the plan. Forward policy activation may use a fresh no-material-change validation; destructive jobs still require the bounded candidate plan and notice windows in DL-18. A restart or a saved browser page cannot resurrect expired approval. Prospective unattended retention may use only the finite, reviewed standing authorization in DL-18: the console shows its exact policy/scope/envelope, budgets, expiry and every generated run plan. Service execution under that delegation is labeled as such, not as a new human approval. Retroactive deletion and hold removal remain human-plan-only. Envelope violations pause rather than silently enlarge authority.

**AC-08.** Scheduled activation must display exact site-local and UTC times, remaining notices, prerequisite health and cancel/pause options. Publish an atomic active-policy revision with history and a clear effective date. Rollback restores a previous **configuration revision** only after a fresh preview; it cannot undelete purged data or revive permissions/session tokens. Post-change verification compares observed counters/actions against the approved plan, flags deviations and pauses subsequent destructive batches. An operator can pause growth-producing jobs or destructive jobs separately while keeping authorized reads/export/cleanup available.

## Operational information architecture

**AC-09.** Provide these first-class, deep-linkable pages; every card links to an actionable detail view and every number has unit, measurement window, source and freshness:

| View | Minimum information | Safe actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overview | Overall/degraded/unknown state, incidents, capacity forecast, largest operational risks, pending approvals/jobs, last verified restore | Inspect, acknowledge with owner/deadline, open runbook |
| Storage and quotas | Physical filesystem/DB/index/WAL/temp/assets/logs/backups, free bytes/inodes, reserve, logical attribution, trash/held data, reservations, reclaimable vs merely hidden bytes | Dry-run cleanup, exception request/review, reconcile meters |
| Users and workspaces | Active/disabled accounts, grants, direct vs inherited/subtree consumption, orphaned stewardship, usage trends, current exceptions | Audited repair/transfer, review limits; no content bypass |
| Activity and performance | Active sessions/sockets, concurrency, operation rates, p50/p95/p99 latency, failed writes, upload/download volumes, admission denials, queue waits | Bounded drilldown, compare periods, identify saturation |
| Lifecycle and archives | Upcoming expiry, notices, trash, holds, purge lag, export/import/view jobs, bytes, reservations, validation status | Preview, approve, pause/cancel/retry at safe checkpoints |
| Policy center | Effective policy, inheritance, drafts/diffs, scenarios, approvals, schedules, exceptions and history | Validated change workflow only |
| Errors and warnings | Structured incidents/events, severity, component, stable code, correlation/job ID, recurrence, impact, first/last occurrence and guidance | Investigate, assign, bounded retry, escalation |
| Backups and recovery | Scheduled/actual runs, manifests/digests, byte counts, protected destination alias, recovery points, restore verification age/duration, failures, retention/expiry | Trigger bounded verified backup, inspect sanitized logs, isolated restore plan |
| Audit and security | Privileged actions, policy/permission changes, authentication anomalies, export/purge outcomes, audit gaps | Filter, protected report, review; no erasing own trail |
| System health | CPU/memory/disk/inodes, DB connections/locks/slow-query aggregates, WAL/replication state if supported, process/version/migrations, worker heartbeat, scheduler lag, proxy/socket failures | Open a named runbook; unsupported metrics remain explicitly unsupported |

Backup locations are approved aliases. The browser may select a provisioned alias and retention policy but cannot supply arbitrary shell commands, filesystem paths, credentials or outbound URLs. Backups and restores run under a constrained service, never by granting the web application unrestricted shell/root access. An import, backup or archive status must not expose database connection strings or raw sensitive dump logs.

**AC-10.** Every dashboard must distinguish `healthy`, `degraded`, `unavailable`, `stale`, `unknown`, and `unsupported`. No metric samples is not zero usage; a configured backup schedule is not a successful backup; a successful backup is not a verified restore. Mark desired policy vs observed running revision separately. Default health/capacity samples must refresh within 60 seconds, job progress within 10 seconds when connected, and detailed quota attribution within 5 minutes; show actual lag and disable freshness-dependent approvals when the promised bound is missed. These are future delivery targets, not evidence about current telemetry.

## Analytics and resource protection

**AC-11.** Provide privacy-conscious usage patterns by approved time window, feature and scope: active accounts (distinct authenticated users with at least one successful action), peak concurrent sessions/sockets, successful/failed operation counts, file sizes and type distributions, storage growth, repeated admission failures, export/import frequency, cleanup lag and a capacity forecast with confidence bounds. Separate scheduled jobs/health probes/retries from user activity; retries of one idempotent action must not inflate successful activity. Distinguish event time vs ingestion time and keep UTC buckets with site-local display and daylight-saving-safe comparisons.

**AC-12.** Routine trends use bounded aggregates, not copies of content. Do not collect personal note text, message bodies, keystrokes, raw search queries, browser fingerprints or external analytics. Individual operational drilldown requires a specific capability, purpose and audit; aggregate trends should suppress cohorts below a configurable threshold (initial 5), marking suppressed/insufficient samples rather than showing zero or enabling differencing through tiny filters. Necessary exact quota enforcement/repair counts remain available to authorized operators as operational metadata, separately from optional behavioral analytics. Detailed/aggregate telemetry retention follows reviewed policy. Forecasts must disclose insufficient history or incomplete sources.

**AC-13.** Sampling, aggregation and report queries must be bounded by time range, cardinality, pagination and execution budget; expensive reports run as jobs. Metrics storage and its own growth are budgeted and monitored. Admin dashboards must not lock production tables for full scans or degrade board fanout to produce decorative real-time charts. Runbooks distinguish a full disk, inodes, retained WAL, stale meters, held data, stalled cleanup and broken backup transport; each has a different remedy. Alert on collectors failing as well as application failures. No mandatory external monitoring service or public network dependency.

## Events, incidents and actionable logs

**AC-14.** Standard events include UTC time, severity, component, stable code, correlation/request/job/attempt IDs, affected authorized scope, policy/software version, stage, retryability and sanitized summary. Job records include the failure boundary, partial state and next safe action. Never log secrets, session cookies, request bodies, archive content, private filenames or note text. Raw sensitive operator diagnostics stay outside the ordinary admin UI; if a privileged diagnostic bundle is supported, require explicit capability, redaction, preview and audited export.

**AC-15.** Filtering must support time range, severity, code, component, actor/scope where permitted, status, job/attempt/correlation ID, active/resolved state and saved views. Provide stable ordering, pagination, total/estimated counts clearly labeled, shareable authorized deep links and sanitized CSV/JSON exports with spreadsheet-formula escaping and export expiry. Correlate one incident with its retries/backup/job history without merging distinct attempts. Acknowledge, assign and resolve with reason; acknowledgement does not mark failing health green or delete source events. Deduplicate bursts with count/first/last timestamps and show suppressed-volume indicators. Notification cooldown/silence must be bounded and auditable; critical audit/storage failure must remain visible.

**AC-16.** Each known warning/error code has an inline, versioned runbook: what failed, practical user impact, evidence to inspect, prerequisites, least-destructive repair, expected success condition, cancellation/rollback limitations and escalation. Buttons must name the actual action and target; never offer a generic Fix all or retry a partially imported database in place. A quota denial suggests cleanup/export/exception; a stale inventory requests reconciliation; a failed restore requires isolation and a fresh empty target, consistent with RECOVERY.md. Unknown errors provide correlation IDs and safe escalation, not a fabricated diagnosis.

## Backup, recovery and resilience controls

**AC-17.** Backup scheduling, retention, protected destination alias, integrity checks and restore-rehearsal cadence are web-configurable through the same review flow. Provisioning the underlying approved storage/credentials remains an operator task. Display backup start/end, source policy/release/schema, recovery-point age, writer-quiescence evidence, digest verification, off-host transfer confirmation and most recent isolated functional restore result. Define warning/error thresholds for missed schedules, verification age, stale recovery points and incomplete transfer. Backup-expiry deletion must require a fresh preview and cannot delete the last verified recovery point needed by an approved destructive plan. Retained backup bytes count against explicit capacity budgets. Ordinary web-admin backup status/job permission does not allow downloading a raw backup bundle containing other users' private content; such custody remains a separately authorized operator workflow outside the content UI.

**AC-18.** A web-triggered restore may only target a preprovisioned isolated alias with empty-target checks, manifest authentication/integrity, explicit approval and job audit. It cannot overwrite live state or switch production routing automatically. Separate approve/execute/verify/activate actions with narrow capabilities. Expose schema compatibility, restored policy/hold state, paused jobs, session invalidation and required functional checks before any activation plan. The console cannot manufacture proof of quiescence, backup verification or restore success from an unchecked acknowledgement. If an external agent must provide evidence, show the signed/identified observation and age; absent evidence blocks the dependent action.

**AC-19.** Provide emergency actions with explicit scope: pause purge, pause imports/exports, disable new uploads, or switch to read-only maintenance while preserving bounded authorized recovery/export operations. Recent reauthentication, reason and audit remain required. Never execute unreviewed purge to resolve disk pressure. A separately provisioned operator recovery path must work when the database/web console is unavailable; record actions for later reconciliation without inventing an unverifiable audit history. Test admission/cleanup/audit behavior under disk exhaustion, DB failure, worker restart, stale metrics and clock drift.

## UI and acceptance

**AC-20.** All pages must be usable with keyboard/screen readers, visible focus, text labels in addition to color, explicit MiB/GiB units, accessible tables and time-zone-aware dates. Preserve filters during navigation, offer clear loading/empty/stale/error states, and avoid reporting success before durable completion. Large reports are cancellable and resumable where supported. Confirmation must summarize affected people/scopes, changed policy, deadlines and irreversible effects in plain language. A competent operator must be able to diagnose a full disk, quota denial, overdue cleanup, failed backup and stale collector from the console without reading server source code.

**AC-21.** Final acceptance requires independent review of capability boundaries, private-metadata handling, preview/approval race conditions, archive/job safety and restore isolation; browser evidence for normal, over-quota, stale, partial-failure and recovery flows; load evidence for dashboard/background-worker impact; and an operator walkthrough following runbooks on synthetic state. No checklist or attractive dashboard alone establishes operational readiness. Record which metrics/actions are unsupported, local-only or untested on the actual site.
